Early access

Everything your PC needs. One light app.

Rakiza Core brings your everyday tools into one calm window: convert, PDFs, archives, disk space, uninstalling and your clouds. Your files never leave your PC.

01 · Files

One workspace for every file job.

Drop your files in, tick the ones you want, pick a tool from the library and run it with one button. Activity shows what's running and where each result was saved.

  • Convert pictures, video, music and Office files
  • Merge, split, compress and lock PDFs
  • Make and open ZIP, RAR, 7z and more
  • Rename hundreds of files at once, with undo
02 · Convert

Hold Shift, drag, drop. Done.

Hold Shift while you drag any file in File Explorer, and a wheel of formats opens at your cursor. Drop it on one and the copy appears next to the original, which is never changed.

  • Resize, crop, rotate and shrink to fit
  • Trim video and music, make GIFs and stickers
  • Copy the text out of any picture
  • Also from the right-click menu
03 · PDF

Arrange PDFs by dragging their pages.

Put several PDFs on one board, drag pages into order, turn or remove them, and save. You can also change what's on a page, add bookmarks and number the pages.

  • Merge, split and reorder
  • Pictures to PDF, PDF to pictures
  • Make big PDFs small enough to send
04 · Space

See what's filling your drive.

A map shows every folder by its size, down to the files. Free up space safely: anything you remove from the map goes to the Recycle Bin.

  • Clean up temporary files, caches and old installers
  • Find duplicate files and similar photos
  • “See what fills this” right from File Explorer
05 · Uninstaller

Remove programs, and what they leave behind.

Uninstall a program, then clear the folders, shortcuts and registry entries it left. Everything removed is kept as a backup for 30 days, so you can put it back.

  • Desktop and Microsoft Store apps in one list
  • Sizes, install dates and when you last used them
06 · Cloud

All your clouds in one window.

OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive and iCloud, found by themselves. Search every cloud at once and drag files from one to another. Files go straight between your PC and the service.

  • Sign in to as many accounts as you like
  • Your password is never seen by Rakiza Core

Recent releases

2.2.0November 9, 2026

New

  • Files has a new look, made after your feedback: a clear file list with tick boxes, type, size and status; a tool library right under it with categories and pinned tools; and a side panel where you pick a tool, set it up and run it with one big button.
  • Merge PDFs right in Files: drag them into order, name the result and pick where it's saved.

  • In the sidebar, Computer now comes before Cloud. If you reordered your sidebar, your order stays.
2.1.0November 8, 2026

New

  • Files is now one workspace: add files or folders and see them in one list, and the side panel shows only the tools that fit what you selected, with their settings right there. Everything you run shows its progress and results at the bottom, and All tools lets you search, pin and switch tools on or off.
  • PDF reader: PDFs open in tabs, with two-page and full-screen views, dark pages, find and copy, and a side panel for pages, bookmarks and comments. Read aloud uses Windows voices, Snapshot copies part of a page as a picture, and Measure shows distances.
  • PDF editor: Fill in and sign (tick, cross, date, initials), stamps (Approved, Draft, Confidential or your own words), and real form boxes to type in or tick.
  • PDF find and cover: phone numbers, emails, ID numbers, IBANs and words you choose are found and truly removed from the file.
  • New PDF tools: Header and footer (page number, page count, date, file name, background colour), PDF to Excel (tables become cells, numbers stay numbers) and PDF to PowerPoint. No Office needed.

Fixes

  • Uses much less memory: Space really lets go of a drive's map once you leave it (it was kept for as long as the window stayed open), and a window left open behind other programs gives its memory back to Windows after two minutes.
  • Space: folders opened deep in the list keep room for their names, and nothing is drawn over the Size column any more.
  • Check: a code pasted with a file name that has md5 or sha256 in it is read, and so is a list of codes saved by PowerShell or certutil.
  • Backup: a folder of yours elsewhere on the backup drive is kept safe, a half copy left when the PC went off is cleared, and every folder left empty is taken out.
  • File Explorer's right-click menu is put right again everywhere if another copy of Rakiza Core wrote over it, including the entry for every kind of file.
  • The PDF editor no longer waits forever, now and then, when opening a small file.
  • Updates: when GitHub is busy for a moment, the update tries again a few times instead of stopping.
2.0.1November 6, 2026

Changed

  • A calmer look: buttons and highlights are now Ink (black on light, near-white on dark), so the logo is the one colour that stands out, and Rakiza Labs under the name is quiet grey. Want teal back? Settings, Look, Teal.
  • Home: clearer widgets. Each size is now one button on the widget, drawn as the part of the page it takes, and "Add a widget" sits next to Done. The widget gallery shows each tool's own icon and previews a widget on a piece of the page, and it stays open so you can add several in a row. Sections that are resting for now take one line instead of whole cards, and Recent activity only shows once there's something in it.
  • The sidebar: an open section shuts again, and sections can be dragged into your own order.

Fixes

  • Space uses far less memory. After a look at a big drive, 2.0.0 could hold over a gigabyte, which made the whole app feel heavy. A look now takes about 40% less at its peak, and Space lets go of its map five minutes after you move to another page.
  • Updating from 1.19 or earlier keeps the Rakiza Core icon on your desktop. Updating to 2.0.0 took away an icon that was on your own desktop (not the one shared by everyone on the PC) and didn't put the new one back.
  • Archives: sorting right after clearing the search shows every file again. Before, only what you had searched for stayed shown, and Select all took just that.
  • Updates: while a full-screen game or video was on, the check for a new version could repeat every 15 minutes, and more often over time. Now it checks once every 15 minutes until the game ends, then updates.
  • Home: "What Rakiza Core did" shows its numbers again, and shortcuts to Downloads and the other folders say "Folder" instead of the whole path.
  • About 750 MB less memory is held after Space or a picture tool has run.
  • Rename: Undo gives every name back, including swapped names, a chain (1 to 2, 2 to 3) and a change of capital letters only. Before, these said "Couldn't undo".
  • Check: a whole line copied from a list of codes is understood, and if one file in a list can't be read, the others are still checked.
  • Backup: old copies past their days are cleared before copying, a file too big for the space left is skipped while the rest is still copied, and an automatic backup only goes to the drive that already holds it.
  • File Explorer's right-click menu is put right again if another copy of Rakiza Core wrote over it.
  • Space and the Uninstaller explain a job that failed in plain words, not Windows' error text.
  • Closing Rakiza Core for an update no longer logs an error.

Questions

Good to know

Which Windows do I need?

Windows 10 or Windows 11, 64-bit.

Why does Windows say “Windows protected your PC”?

Rakiza Core isn't code-signed yet, so Windows doesn't recognise it. Click More info, then Run anyway.

Each release lists SHA-256 checksums, so you can check that your download is the real file.

Does it send anything over the internet?

Only a check for its own updates on GitHub (you can turn it off), and your cloud accounts if you choose to sign in. No statistics, no tracking. Read the privacy policy.

What does “early access” mean?

Rakiza Core works today, and we use it every day, but we are still polishing it. A few tools are resting while we focus on the essentials; they come back in later updates.

Does it ask for administrator rights?

Rakiza Core installs in Program Files for everyone on the PC, so Windows asks for permission when you install it.

How do I remove it?

Like any program, from Windows Settings, Apps. It offers to delete its settings too.

Where do I report a problem or suggest something?

On GitHub. We read everything.
