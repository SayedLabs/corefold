Rakiza Core is a free Windows app made by Rakiza Labs. In short: your files stay on your PC, there are no Rakiza Core accounts, no ads and no tracking, and we have no server that could collect anything. Rakiza Core only goes online by itself to check for its own updates. If you choose to use Rakiza Core Cloud, it works with the cloud folders already on your PC, or talks straight to your own OneDrive or Dropbox if you sign in, and to nobody else.

Your files

Everything Rakiza Core does to your files (converting, shrinking, removing backgrounds, reading text, opening archives and emails, taking screenshots, uninstalling apps) happens on your own PC. Nothing is uploaded, to us or to anyone else, unless you upload it yourself with Rakiza Core Cloud.

Rakiza Core Cloud (OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive and iCloud)

Cloud shows your clouds in one window. It's optional, and it works in two ways.

Folders on your PC. Cloud finds the OneDrive, Dropbox, Google Drive and iCloud folders that those companies' own apps keep on your PC. It finds them by reading what those apps write on your PC (OneDrive's settings in the Windows registry, Dropbox's info.json, Google Drive's drive). For these folders Rakiza Core doesn't go online at all: it opens, copies, renames and deletes files in them on your PC, and the company's own app sends the changes to its service, as it does for anything you save there. Deleted files go to the Recycle Bin. Nothing about these folders is kept by Rakiza Core.

Signing in online (Google Drive, OneDrive and Dropbox), when your copy of Rakiza Core offers it. Nothing of theirs needs to be installed, and you can sign in to as many accounts as you like, several of the same service too. Nothing happens until you sign in to an account.

  • Signing in happens on Google's, Microsoft's or Dropbox's own page, in your web browser. Rakiza Core never sees your password. While you sign in, Rakiza Core listens on a local address on your own PC (http://localhost or http://127.0.0.1) to receive the answer; nothing outside your PC can reach it.
  • What Rakiza Core asks for: permission to see, add, change and delete your files, to see your name, email address and how much space you use, and to stay signed in so you don't have to sign in every time.
  • What Rakiza Core keeps: the key the service gives back (not your password), plus your name, email address and storage use so the account can be shown. They're kept only on your PC, in %LOCALAPPDATA%\Rakiza Core\Cloud, locked with Windows' own encryption so only your Windows account can unlock them.
  • Your files go straight between your PC and the service. They don't pass through Rakiza Labs or anyone else. Copying from one cloud to another goes through your PC too.
  • Files you open from Cloud are downloaded to a folder on your PC (%LOCALAPPDATA%\Rakiza Core\Opened cloud files) so the right app can open them. Changes stay on your PC until you upload the file again; a file you changed is never cleared away, also not when Rakiza Core is removed. Google Docs, Sheets and Slides open on Google's website instead.
  • Deleting an online file moves it to the service's own bin or deleted files, where you can put it back for a while. Rakiza Core never deletes anything there for good.
  • Sign out deletes Rakiza Core's key for that account from your PC, and for Google and Dropbox also asks them to cancel it. To also take back the permission on the service's side, use your Google account's connections, your Microsoft account's app permissions (work or school accounts: My Apps) or Dropbox's connected apps.

Your files on Google Drive, OneDrive and Dropbox are covered by those companies' own policies: Google's privacy policy, Microsoft's privacy statement and Dropbox's privacy policy.

Rakiza Core's use of information received from Google APIs adheres to the Google API Services User Data Policy, including the Limited Use requirements: what Rakiza Core gets from Google Drive is only used to show and work with your files on your PC, it's never sent to Rakiza Labs or anyone else, and it's never used for ads.

Rakiza Core doesn't send usage statistics, crash reports or any information about your files, and it doesn't count its users. If a future version offers to send a crash report, it will ask you first and show you what would be sent.

Rakiza Core Network shows which programs are connected and where, by reading what Windows already knows on your PC (its list of connections and the names it looked up lately). It never asks a server about them, and nothing it shows is sent anywhere.

Emails you open in Rakiza Core can't reach the internet either: pictures from the internet are blocked, so the sender can't tell you opened the email.

What stays on your PC

Rakiza Core keeps these in your user folders (%APPDATA%\Rakiza Core and %LOCALAPPDATA%\Rakiza Core), and nowhere else:

  • your settings;
  • a list of recent conversions (you can clear it in History);
  • your clipboard history, if you use Clipboard (copies that password managers mark as private are never kept);
  • your Cloud accounts' keys, locked as described above;
  • the speed tests you ran in Network (speeds and the city of the test server, never your address), and which programs it has seen using the internet before, to point out new ones;
  • a small log of what the app did, to help find problems, which never leaves your PC unless you send it;
  • the backups made when you uninstall apps with Rakiza Core.

When you uninstall Rakiza Core, it offers to delete all of this. The backups of uninstalled apps are kept, because they're yours; you can delete them yourself.

This website

This site is hosted by GitHub Pages, which may keep logs of visits (like your IP address) for security. The site itself has no cookies, analytics or trackers, and its fonts and pictures come from the site itself, not from other companies.

Download buttons ask GitHub, which hosts both this site and Rakiza Core's releases, for the newest version, so the button starts the right file and shows its size. If you pick a language or light or dark on this site, that choice is remembered in your own browser only.

Children

Rakiza Core doesn't collect personal information from anyone, including children.

Your choices

Because Rakiza Labs doesn't collect or keep any information about you, there's nothing for us to show, change or delete. Everything Rakiza Core keeps is on your own PC, where you can delete it. For files and accounts on OneDrive or Dropbox, use those services' own settings.
